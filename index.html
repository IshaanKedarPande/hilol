    03

    Method

    Every record goes through the same four steps, written in Python with pandas and SciPy. The code is on GitHub.

    1. Step 1

      Collect

      One CSV per variable and place: a monthly value from January 2006 to January 2026.

    2. Step 2

      Clean

      Months stored as −9999 are missing. Up to two missing months in a row are filled in from their neighbours; anything still missing is dropped.

    3. Step 3

      Take out the seasons

      Subtract the long-term average for each calendar month. What's left, the anomaly, says whether a month was above or below normal for that time of year.

    4. Step 4

      Fit and test

      Fit a straight line through the anomalies (linear regression). The slope is the rate of change. A t-test on the slope gives a p-value; below 0.05 counts as significant.

    Glossary

    Trend
    A steady, long-term change in one direction that continues underneath the short-term ups and downs.
    Anomaly
    How far a month's value is above (+) or below (−) the usual value for that calendar month. Using anomalies removes the summer–winter cycle so it doesn't hide a trend.
    Rate of change
    The slope of the fitted line: how much the variable changes per year on average, in its own units (for example kelvin per year).
    Linear regression
    The method that draws the single straight line that fits the points as closely as possible. It's the dashed line on the explorer charts.
    p-value
    If there were really no trend, how likely is it that random month-to-month noise would produce a slope at least this steep? Small means “unlikely to be chance alone”.
    Statistically significant
    We use p < 0.05. Significant does not mean large or important, and not significant does not prove nothing is changing.
    95% confidence interval
    A range of plausible values for the true rate of change. If the whole range is above zero (or below zero), the direction is clear.
    R² (R-squared)
    The share of the month-to-month variation the straight line explains, from 0 to 1. A trend can be significant with a small R², because over 240 months even a gentle slope adds up.
    05

    What this can and can't tell you

    It can tell you

    • Which way each variable has moved at each place since 2006, and roughly how fast, in real units per year.
    • Whether a straight-line trend stands out from the month-to-month noise.
    • That the same variable can move in opposite directions in different places.

    It can't tell you

    • Why something changed. A trend is a clue, not a cause.
    • What happens next. Twenty years is short in climate terms, and trends can speed up, slow down or reverse.
    • Anything about cycles or curved changes. We only test for straight lines.
    • Exactly what a ground station would read. These are satellite and model values for a location.

    Things we know are imperfect

    • Neighbouring months tend to resemble each other. Our test assumes they don't, so treat p-values close to 0.05 with extra caution.
    • Some records have gaps. The explorer says how many months are missing for each place and shades the gaps on the chart.
    • A result that isn't significant now could become significant with a longer record.
